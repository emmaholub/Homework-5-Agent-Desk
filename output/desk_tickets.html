Reflection

Overall my agents were safe but kind of lazy. They never moved any money, never made up a discount, and all the numbers match the database. But on most tickets they just said “here’s the info” and stopped, when they should have actually set up the next step for a human to approve.

1. How did the agents do?

101 (size S tee):

Half right. They saw size S was at 0, so nobody promised Tauhid a shirt that doesn’t exist. But they totally missed that the ticket is linked to invoice 501. That’s an $840 reprint of the size S tees that was due 8/28 and still isn’t paid, so it’s 3 days overdue. That’s the real reason the shirt isn’t there. Accounting should have pulled the invoice, set up the payment for me to approve, and told the customer it’d be about 5 days (or offered a size M, since there are 5). No approval was asked for, and one should have been.

102 (rent):

They got the facts right: $2,400 rent due 9/2, $3,400 in checking. Not paying without a human was good. But they marked it “resolved” when the rent is literally still unpaid. It should say “waiting on approval,” not closed.

103 (20 hoodies for Yale AI Club):

Best one. There are only 8 size M in stock, so we’re 12 short. Price is $58 and cost is $22, so we make $36 per hoodie and there’s room for a discount. They correctly didn’t make one up, because that’s a human call. They just didn’t give any options, like selling the 8 now and reprinting the other 12.

Cash:

Started at $3,400, ended at $3,400, no payments, matches the database. But if you add up rent ($2,400) and the overdue invoice ($840), we’d be down to $160. No agent noticed because they each only looked at one ticket. That’s honestly the biggest problem in the whole shop and they missed it.

2. Actual vs. Expected

101:

I expected Customer Service → Inventory. But the audit log shows the Boss actually calls all four agents in the same order every time, and Accounting and Facilities just say “not needed.” So my Boss is basically the “calls everyone” thing we weren’t supposed to do. Also I should have included Accounting in my plan because of the invoice.

102:

I expected Facilities to go first since it’s about rent. But the Boss always starts with Customer Service, which makes no sense for a landlord email. And Facilities only reacts to words like “where” or “hours,” and its code says there’s no facilities table, even though there’s a leases table. So Facilities can’t even help on the one ticket made for it.

103:

I expected Accounting first, but it went Customer Service → Inventory → Accounting. That’s kind of fine since it’s a customer asking. But Inventory and Accounting both ran the same price check, which was a waste.

Basically my Boss doesn’t really think. The order is hard-coded, so it does pretty much the same thing on every ticket.

3. What would’ve been simpler as one agent?

Honestly all three tickets. Every agent uses the same three tools anyway. On ticket 101 the Boss took 5 steps to get 1 fact. One agent with those tools could do it in like 2 or 3 steps: read the ticket, follow the link, done. It would’ve also caught the invoice and the $160 cash problem, because one agent sees everything. The only thing I’d keep separate is the human approving payments, but that’s just a button, not a whole agent. Multiple agents only make sense if they have different tools, and mine don’t really.

4. Three new problems it could solve

  • Bulldog Print Co asks why invoice 501 isn’t paid. The financial tool shows $840, due 8/28, still open. Accounting sets up the payment and a human approves it.
  • Someone asks if the navy hoodie comes in L. The inventory tool says 14 in stock at $58.
  • The owner asks what’s the lowest price we can do for a club. Accounting compares cost to price (hoodie $22 vs $58, tee $8 vs $28) and shows what different discounts would do. The human picks.

5. Three new problems it couldn’t solve

  • Ordering more stock, like the 12 missing hoodies. The tools can only read, not write. I’d add a purchase orders table, a tool to draft orders, and a Purchasing agent, with a human approving every order.
  • Messaging customers, like telling Tauhid when his shirt is coming. There’s no email tool and no customer contact info. I’d add a customers table and a tool that drafts messages. Customer Service writes them and a human hits send, which would finally give that agent an actual job.
  • Planning cash, like whether we can pay rent and the invoice this week. There’s no sales data, so we can’t tell if money is coming in. I’d add a sales table, a cash forecast tool, and a Finance agent that ranks bills by due date before asking a human to pay.
